DTC P1F0B: AWD Power Supply Voltage: Notes

1. - 

DTC detecting condition: 

Immediately at fault recognition

Trouble symptom: 

Driveability getting worse.

Wiring diagram: 

AWD control system Refer to WIRING SYSTEM (CROSSTREK MODEL (HEV))>AWD CONTROL SYSTEM .

Courtesy of SUBARU OF AMERICA, INC.
  1. CHECK DATA MONITOR  .

    Read the data of [Main Power Supply Voltage] using Subaru Select Monitor. Refer to AWD CONTROL (DIAGNOSTICS)>DATA MONITOR .

    Is the [Main Power Supply Voltage] less than 9.5 V?

    Yes:  Go to  2.

    No:  Current condition is normal. Reproduce the failure, and then perform the diagnosis again.

  2. CHECK HARNESS  .
    1. Turn the ignition switch to OFF.
    2. Disconnect the AWD control module connector.
    3. Remove the AWD control module relay.
    4. Measure the resistance between AWD control module connector and relay holder.

      Connector & terminal 

      (B648) No. 2 - (B225) No. 38:

      (B648) No. 15 - (B225) No. 38:

    Is the resistance less than 1 Ω?

    Yes:  Go to  3.

    No:  Repair the open circuit of harness.

  3. CHECK RELAY POWER SUPPLY  .

    Measure the resistance between AWD control module connector and chassis ground.

    Connector & terminal 

    (B648) No. 2 - Chassis ground:

    (B648) No. 15 - Chassis ground:

    Is the resistance 1 MΩ or more?

    Yes:  Go to  4.

    No:  Repair the short circuit of harness.

  4. CHECK RELAY POWER SUPPLY  .

    Measure the voltage between relay holder and chassis ground.

    Connector & terminal 

    (B225) No. 35 (+) - Chassis ground (-):

    (B225) No. 37 (+) - Chassis ground (-):

    Is the voltage 10 V or more?

    Yes:  Go to  5.

    No:  Repair the open or short circuit of harness.

  5. CHECK AWD control module RELAY  .
    1. Replace AWD control module relay.
    2. Connect the AWD control module connector.
    3. Read the data of [Main Power Supply Voltage] using Subaru Select Monitor. Refer to AWD CONTROL (DIAGNOSTICS)>DATA MONITOR .

    Is the [Main Power Supply Voltage] 9.5 V or more?

    Yes:  Check for poor contact of AWD control module connector. Replace the AWD control module if no fault is found. Refer to CONTINUOUSLY VARIABLE TRANSMISSION (TH2A)>AWD CONTROL MODULE .

    No:  The original AWD control module relay is defective.
