Check Power Supply Circuit

Do you like it dark? Easier on the eyes.

Trouble causes: 

  • Pedestrian alert system circuit malfunction
  • Pedestrian alert control module malfunction

Wiring diagram: 

Pedestrian alert system Ref. to WIRING SYSTEM (CROSSTREK MODEL (HEV))>PEDESTRIAN ALERT SYSTEM>WIRING DIAGRAM .

Courtesy of SUBARU OF AMERICA, INC.
CAUTION: Before performing diagnosis, refer to "CAUTION" in "General Description". Ref. to PEDESTRIAN ALERT (DIAGNOSTICS)>GENERAL DESCRIPTION>CAUTION .
  1. CHECK FUSE. 
    1. Turn the ignition switch to OFF.
    2. Check the fuse. Ref. to PEDESTRIAN ALERT SYSTEM>RELAY AND FUSE .

      Is the fuse OK?

      Yes:  Go to  2.

      No:  Replace the fuse. When the replaced fuse is blown immediately, repair the short circuit.

  2. CHECK HARNESS. 
    1. Disconnect the pedestrian alert control module connector.
    2. Using the tester, measure the voltage between pedestrian alert control module connector and chassis ground.

      Connector & terminal 

      (B641) No. 1 (+) - Chassis ground (-):

      Is the voltage 10 V or more?

      Yes:  Go to  3.

      No:  Repair or replace the open circuit of harness.

  3. CHECK HARNESS. 

    Using the tester, measure the resistance between pedestrian alert control module connector and chassis ground.

    Connector & terminal 

    (B641) No. 6 - Chassis ground:

    Is the resistance less than 1 Ω?

    Yes:  Go to  4.

    No:  Repair or replace the open circuit of harness.

  4. CHECK DATA MONITOR. 
    1. Turn the ignition switch to ON.
    2. Using the Subaru Select Monitor, check the following items in the [Data monitor] of [Pedestrian Alert]. Ref. to PEDESTRIAN ALERT (DIAGNOSTICS)>DATA MONITOR .
      • Voltage of IGN

      Is the voltage 10 V or more when the ignition switch is ON?

      Yes:  Even if abnormality is detected, the circuit has returned to a normal condition at this time. Reproduce the failure, and then perform the diagnosis again.

      NOTE: In this case, temporary poor contact of connector, temporal open or short circuit of harness may be the cause.

      No:  Replace the pedestrian alert control module. Ref. to PEDESTRIAN ALERT SYSTEM>PEDESTRIAN ALERT CONTROL UNIT .
